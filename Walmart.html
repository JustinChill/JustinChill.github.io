Sprawl-Mart

The Diffusion of Wal-Mart

Wal the way

Wal-Mart stores opened, by decade

Every dot is a Wal-Mart, in the decade it opened. The 15 stores of the 1960s were all within 260 miles of Bentonville, Arkansas. In the 1970s, the typical new one was 228 miles away. In the 1980s it was 494, and in the 1990s, when they got to both coasts, 886.

There’s a reason for growing that way, as explained in a 2011 master’s thesis. If stores are close to one another, they’re close to the same distribution center, and on average Wal-Mart saves around $3,500 a year per store by locating just one mile closer to one.

The beast from Bentonville

Wal-Mart stores and distribution centers, 1962–2006

By January 2006, two-thirds of the stores were within 100 miles of a distribution center, in a straight line.

Target, K-Mart, and Wal-Mart each opened their first discount store in 1962. K-Mart’s was outside Detroit and Target’s was outside Minneapolis. Wal-Mart’s was in Rogers, Arkansas, a small town down the road from Bentonville.

The first two went after big cities, and they grew quickly. Wal-Mart stayed in small towns near home, and let the towns get farther and farther away.

Scale by the numbers

Wal-Mart stores opened, by year

The busiest year was 1990, with 174. The spike in 1981 is mostly Kuhn’s Big K, a Tennessee chain whose 92 stores Wal-Mart bought that year. Holmes’s file has 82 stores opening on one day, July 4.
