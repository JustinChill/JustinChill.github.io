Space Waste

Debris left on the Moon’s surface

Lunar Missions

Shown above are planetary missions that have impacted the Moon, either as complete spacecraft or as jettisoned components. Humans are remarkably proficient at generating and distributing trash — there are nearly 100 bags of human waste left behind on the Moon — and with countries around the globe planning missions to return, there will be a lot more space waste, both on the lunar surface and in Earth’s orbit.

In August 2023, Russia’s Luna-25 probe crashed into the Moon’s surface. Shortly after, India’s Chandrayaan-3 mission successfully landed in the southern polar region, making India the fourth country to land on the Moon.

Japan became the fifth in January 2024, when its SLIM lander touched down on its nose. Private companies have joined the traffic too: Intuitive Machines’ Odysseus became the first commercial lander to reach the surface in February 2024, and Firefly’s Blue Ghost made the first fully successful commercial landing in March 2025, while both of ispace’s landers crashed, in 2023 and 2025.
