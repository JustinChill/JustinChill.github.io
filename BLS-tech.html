Tech Turns the Corner

A snapshot from December 2025

Written at a moment. This post dates from December 2025 and used early BLS figures, which showed tech with 1.6k more jobs in August 2025 than a year before. The BLS has since revised them, and that gain is now a loss of 23.5k. The numbers below are the revised ones, as published in November 2026, and the chart stops where the post did.

Year-over-Year Change in US Tech Employment

The US tech sector looked like it had stopped bleeding. The first figures for August 2025 showed a year-over-year net gain, the first since August 2023, by a razor-thin +1.6k jobs. The revised count is −23.5k. What held up is that the losses had eased: in March 2024, the worst month, the sector was down 74.4k.

Here is how August 2025 compared with a year earlier, by industry:

The Growth Engines: Software & Search

The growth there was came from high-margin, scalable tech products.

  • Software Publishers: The undisputed heavy lifter. Employment sat at 656.3k, up 14.6k year-over-year.
  • Streaming & Social Networks: Up 4.4k.
  • Web Search & Portals: Up 2.9k, on a small base.

The Anchor: Services & Consulting

  • IT Services & Consulting: Custom Programming was down 16.2k and Systems Design 27.3k. Both were well below their 2023 peaks, by 61.6k and 38.2k. Companies were buying software, not the consultants to install it.
  • Computing Infrastructure: Down 1.9k.

Takeaway

In December 2025 the read was that tech had bottomed out: the “Year of Efficiency” cuts were over, replaced by a cautious, product-led stabilization. The revisions don’t bear that out, and neither does what came next. By September 2026 the same six industries were down 72.6k on the year, though that figure is preliminary too.
