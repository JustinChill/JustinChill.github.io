Hogwarts House Cup & the Gryffindor Bias

Why does Gryffindor always win?

The House Cup, 1991–92 🪄

Every student at Hogwarts belongs to one of four houses, and all year the teachers give them points or take them away: for a right answer in class, say, or for being out of bed at midnight. Four giant hourglasses keep the score, filled with rubies for Gryffindor, emeralds for Slytherin, sapphires for Ravenclaw, and diamonds for Hufflepuff. In the books they stand in the Entrance Hall; the films moved them into the Great Hall. At the end of the year, the house with the most points wins the House Cup.

Gryffindor doesn’t always win. When Harry Potter gets there, Slytherin has won the cup six years in a row, and by the end of his first year it has all but won the seventh. At the year-end feast, Slytherin leads Gryffindor by 160 points. Then Dumbledore stands up and gives Gryffindor 170.

Ron and Hermione get 50 each, and Harry gets 60, which makes it a tie. The last 10 go to Neville Longbottom.

While you are at Hogwarts, your triumphs will earn your House points, while any rule-breaking will lose House points. At the end of the year, the House with the most points is awarded the House Cup, a great honor.

Professor Minerva McGonagall

House points given and taken, by book

The books don’t keep score. After the first year there isn’t even a final total. But the Harry Potter Lexicon, a fan site, has logged every time somebody gives or takes points, and how many: 69 times in six books. It has no log for the seventh, which Harry mostly spends somewhere else.

We see the school through Harry, so 62 of the 69 are Gryffindor’s. Slytherin is given points once, and that’s for catching Harry. Hufflepuff is never given any.

A graphic in the New York Times inspired this article. I like how they presented a diverging bar chart to show change and wanted to mimic the style with happier data.

Who gave and took Gryffindor’s points

Gryffindor is given 990 points, and 570 of them are from Dumbledore, all in the last chapters of the first two books. Snape never gives it any. He takes 267, about half of what it loses. McGonagall, its own head of house, takes 150 in one night.

So there is a bias, but it isn’t that Gryffindor never loses points. It loses 527, more than five times as many as the other three put together. It wins because the headmaster goes last. It wins the second year too, with another 400 at the end, and the third. After that, the books don’t say who does.

It takes a great deal of bravery to stand up to our enemies, but just as much to stand up to our friends. I therefore award ten points to Mr. Neville Longbottom.

Albus Dumbledore, Harry Potter and the Sorcerer’s Stone
