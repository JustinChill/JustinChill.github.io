The Big Short

An analysis of failed banks in the US

U.S. bank failures since 2000, sized by assets

Banks fail more often than you’d think. The FDIC’s list has 578 since October 2000, about 21 a year. Most of them went quietly. The one in the middle had $203 million in assets and a name you’ve never heard.

Then there are the others. Silicon Valley Bank failed on March 10, 2023, Signature Bank two days later, and First Republic on May 1. Together the three had $549 billion, more than every bank that failed from 2009 through 2022 put together.

Failures per month

From 2001 through 2007, the country averaged fewer than four a year. Then came the crash. Of the 578 failures, 465, or 80 percent, happened in the five years from 2008 through 2012. The worst year was 2010, with 157, about five times as many as the last 10 years combined.

It has been quiet since. No banks failed in 2018, 2021, or 2022. Two failed in 2024, two in 2025, and six so far in 2026. The largest of those, Nano Banc of Irvine, California, had $736 million.

Silicon Valley Bank ended a run of 868 days. The longest, more than two and a half years, ended 10 months before the Great Recession began.

What is the largest bank failure?

Washington Mutual is still the largest in U.S. history. It had $307 billion when risky mortgages sank it in September 2008, about $473 billion in today’s money. First Republic is second, at $229 billion, or $255 billion today.

Silicon Valley is third ($209 billion) and Signature is fourth ($110 billion). Both were favorites of startups, whose deposits were far over the insurance limit and whose founders could empty an account from a phone. At Silicon Valley, they tried to take out $42 billion in a day.

The banks that failed before and after them were more typical. Almena State Bank in Kansas had $69 million. Heartland Tri-State Bank, also in Kansas, had $139 million, and Citizens Bank of Sac City, Iowa, had $66 million. Silicon Valley Bank was 1,500 to 3,200 times their size. Even the 157 banks that failed in 2010 had less than half of what it had, all put together.

When do banks fail?

On Fridays. Of the 578, 551 (95 percent) failed on a Friday. Only two failed on a weekend: Dollar Savings Bank, on a Saturday in 2004, and Signature Bank, on Sunday, March 12, 2023.

Most banks close for the weekend, and that’s the point. If the FDIC walks in on a Friday evening, it has two days to count the money, find a buyer, and open the doors on Monday under a new name, before anybody has time to panic. It isn’t only about the one bank. A panic at one tends to spread to the next.

Signature couldn’t wait. Customers took out more than $10 billion on the Friday that Silicon Valley failed, and regulators shut it on Sunday night. Barney Frank, who sat on its board, told CNBC that it had already stabilized.
