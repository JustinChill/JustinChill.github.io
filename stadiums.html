Field of dreams

World sports stadiums

Sports venues around the world, sized by capacity

No matter where you go, you can see the profound impact sports have had on each nation. Rich or poor, big or small, every country on Earth has its own stadium where its favorite game is played. Sports are much more than a simple game, of course; in many instances, they are major cultural events that have the undivided attention of millions of people. Sometimes, people's entire identities are formed around their most beloved sports team.

Every team and event needs a venue. While some of these stadiums are quaint and humble, others are astronomical in size, costing hundreds of millions of dollars and often taking years to build. The biggest stadiums in the world serve as shrines to the cultural phenomena that are sport and live entertainment.

This dataset lists the home grounds of 10,341 teams. Some teams share a ground, so that works out to about 9,100 venues in 228 countries and territories. The United States has the most, with 782, followed by Germany (519), Spain (397), the United Kingdom (363) and South Korea (343).

Most of the world’s stadiums are football grounds

Six in ten venues are home to a football team. In the United States and Canada that mostly means American or Canadian football; nearly everywhere else, it’s soccer ⚽.

The United States

The largest venue in the dataset, with 257,325 seats, is the Indianapolis Motor Speedway in Indiana. Racetracks take the top three American spots; the biggest stadium in the data is Bryant–Denny Stadium in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, with 101,821.

American venues by sport

India

Football grounds outnumber cricket grounds in the data, 77 to 60, but cricket has several of India’s biggest venues, including Eden Gardens in Kolkata, which holds 67,549.

Indian venues by sport

South Korea

More than two-thirds of South Korea’s venues fall outside the main team sports. Most are multi-use city stadiums; the rest are halls built for sports such as cycling, swimming, tennis and volleyball.

South Korean venues by sport

Ireland

Ireland’s biggest grounds mostly belong to Gaelic games, a family of sports that includes Gaelic football (a mix of soccer and rugby) and hurling. Croke Park in Dublin, the headquarters of the Gaelic Athletic Association, holds 82,300. The source data files these grounds under rugby, so I’ve relabelled them as Gaelic games.
