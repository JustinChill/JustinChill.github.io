UN Population Projections 2022

The Road to 10 Billion

The United Nations published World Population Prospects, its projection for every country and every year to 2100, in July 2022. The world passed 8 billion people that November. By the UN’s median, it reaches 9 billion in 2037 and 10 billion in 2058, then peaks at 10.43 billion at the end of 2085 and eases to 10.34 billion by the end of the century.

Nobody knows how many children people will have in 2070, so the UN runs a Bayesian hierarchical model and publishes a range. The solid line is the UN’s estimate up to 2021 and the model’s median after that. The shaded bands are its 80% and 95% credible intervals, and they widen with every year: for 2100, the 95% band runs from 8.8 to 12.4 billion.

The UN has since trimmed its forecast. The 2024 edition puts the peak at just under 10.3 billion, in 2084. The charts here use the 2022 edition.

Country Projections

Afghanistan: 41.7 million people at the end of 2022, and a median projection of 111.0 million for 2100. The 95% interval runs from 56.4 million to 242.7 million.

Africa on the Rise

Africa adds 2.5 billion people by 2100, more than the whole world’s net gain of 2.3 billion. The 15 fastest-growing countries and territories are all there. Niger’s population rises more than sixfold, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s more than quadruples.

Elsewhere, populations level off or fall. Asia and Latin America end the century about where they are now, and Europe loses a fifth of its people. China loses almost half, while India grows 7%. In all, 98 of 236 countries and territories are projected to be smaller in 2100 than in 2022, led by American Samoa (−72%) and Jamaica (−69%).
