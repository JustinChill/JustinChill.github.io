Gun Regulation Index

State gun regulations and firearm mortality rates across America

An Era of Progress on Gun Mortality

An article by Patrick Sharkey and Megan Kang in the journal Epidemiology scores each state’s gun regulations with an index. Restrictive laws add to it and permissive laws subtract. The chart sets that score against the state’s gun deaths per 100,000 people in 2016, and is a reproduction of figure 2 in the article.

The line falls by about 0.6 deaths for each point of the index. That’s only a correlation: states differ in more than their gun laws. The study’s own estimate is smaller. Each restrictive law a state passed between 1991 and 2016 was associated with 0.21 fewer gun deaths per 100,000 people. For a state of 10 million, about the size of North Carolina, that comes to roughly 21 lives a year for each law.

Across the 40 states that tightened their laws, the authors estimate that 4,297 gun deaths were averted in 2016 alone, about 11% of that year’s total.

It's worth noting, though, that the study is based on data up to 2016 and that gun laws in many places, including North Carolina, are now more permissive.

Footnotes

  1. Patrick Sharkey and Megan Kang, “The Era of Progress on Gun Mortality,” Epidemiology, November 2023 (abstract). The 95% confidence interval is 0.08 to 0.33. ↩
