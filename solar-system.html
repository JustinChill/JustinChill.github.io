Our solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago. The four planets closest to the Sun — Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars — are called the terrestrial planets because they have solid, rocky surfaces. Two of the outer planets beyond the orbit of Mars — Jupiter and Saturn — are known as gas giants; the more distant Uranus and Neptune are called ice giants.

This visualization shows the different objects orbiting the Sun in our solar system, with the Sun on the left (or at the top on a phone). It is not to scale: distances are compressed on a cube-root scale so that Eris fits on the same page as Mercury, and each object’s size reflects its equatorial radius on a separate scale. Hover over an object, or open the data table, for exact figures.
