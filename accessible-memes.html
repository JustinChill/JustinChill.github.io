Accessible Memes

Memes are for everybody

While memes are usually considered unessential and solely for humor, their use often builds connections within the learning community; therefore, those who cannot glean the full meaning often feel left out.

Fortunately, making memes accessible on the web is easy! The memes on this page are all made using regular HTML & CSS, leveraging the power of layout styles to format everything. They are accessible to screen readers (offering up a description for each image) and can be easily edited or translated.

The Basics Of Making Memes Accessible

  1. Know your meme:
    1. Memes that rely heavily on knowledge of obscure source material can be inaccessible for anyone unfamiliar with the meme's original context.
    2. Consider simplifying or providing background context when describing them.
  2. Describing with Alt-Text: explain both the visual context and the text clearly.
  3. Alt-Text needs to convey both the imagery and the message to be fully accessible.
  4. Repetition & Common Formats:
    1. Using standard base templates makes it easier for readers and screen reader users to digest your content.
    2. Using popular base memes increases familiarity and accessibility across audiences.

Inspiration for this post from Adrian Roselli. He called attention to common web pitfalls and offered accessible HTML/CSS solutions. Try making and sharing your own accessible memes!
